# Compendium conventions

The compendium is a reference organised by subject. The `\qfpart` declarations in `compendium.tex` are the only declaration of Parts, sections and their order, and they must match the pinned guide's `learning_parts`. `compendium.tex` includes each existing `<section-key>/section.tex` in that order and prints a Part heading only when one of its sections exists. Each chapter wrapper contains its heading (the guide section's name), its label, a `\qfpathway{pathway-key}{Name}` line for each specialist pathway that has entries, in the guide's `specialist_order` for that section and with the guide's name, and under each pathway its topic inputs, each input once. `\qfpathway` prints a `\section` labelled `pathway:<pathway-key>`; a bare `\section` is not used. A pathway with several topic files gives each a `\subsection` heading; the only topic file of a pathway has no heading, so the pathway name is not repeated; `\subsubsection` divides a topic. A pathway without entries of its own is not printed, and material that serves several pathways stays in one canonical home and is cross-referenced from the others. Every topic file, and so every entry, sits under the pathway whose outcome covers it; a chapter may open with brief prose in its wrapper, but holds no unnumbered collection of entries. The mapping and its history are recorded in `../subsection_alignment_plan.md`; entries added afterwards are mapped in their batch records (`../content_development_plan.md` for `return-predictability`). Create a wrapper together with its first substantive topic file and its overview note in `notes/pathways/`; declared sections without content need no empty chapter.

## Content

Use the guide's eleven entry types and their label prefixes. Every entry needs a descriptive title, a unique label, a Statement field and citations with locators. Conditions, Notation, Practice, Pitfalls, See also and Further reading should contain information useful to a reader of the subject.

Explain the concept directly. Aim for one idea and roughly 80–140 words per paragraph; split long fields at topic changes and sentence boundaries. Preserve assumptions, units, date ranges, source limitations and distinctions between illustrative calculations and empirical evidence. Explain an incorrect formula or interpretation only when the correction teaches a material distinction. Omit editing narratives, study progress, retrieval diaries and lists of keywords: do not record what was or was not read, checked or compared, how a source was obtained, which script reproduced a number, or which version of an entry came first. A version note belongs in an entry only when it changes the locators (for example, page numbers that refer to a working paper).

Source-review provenance is stored by entry label in `notes/entry-evidence.json`, with detailed research records in `notes/source-review-records.md`. These records do not imply that a claim is independently verified. Keep important historical or jurisdictional limits in the entry's Conditions even when also recorded outside the PDF.

The full working of a derivation belongs in the model reference (`../model-derivations/`); an entry's proof sketch links to it with `\qfderivation{drv:<name>}`, and the reference links back with `\qfentrylink{<label>}`. Both resolve through label-named PDF destinations, so neither document cites the other's page numbers.

Use subject names for files and operations for functions. Update references when renaming an artefact. Preserve numerical inputs, results and historical evidence identities; a filename change is not a new research run.

## Typography

The document uses `extbook` at 8 pt, with Source Sans Pro text, Source Code Pro identifiers and matching sans-serif mathematics (`newtxsf`). Part, chapter, section, subsection and subsubsection headings use 17, 14, 11, 9 and 8 pt respectively. The contents list Parts, chapters and sections (the pathways); PDF bookmarks also show subsections and subsubsections, and the back matter starts at the bookmark root. The title page follows the class's scaled type sizes. Lists have no extra vertical space between items or paragraphs within items. The LuaTeX line breaker in `typography.lua` keeps at least five words on the last line of every multi-line paragraph and list item: it first breaks the paragraph with its last five words tied, and falls back to the normal break if that would overfill a line or stretch one beyond the normal tolerance. Where the fallback still leaves a short last line, reword the paragraph; never use manual line breaks or ragged-right setting to move words. Single-line items and table cells keep their natural layout; widow/orphan penalties keep isolated lines off page boundaries, and an entry's title is kept on the page of its first field. The rules are in `preamble.tex`; avoid local spacing overrides unless a particular table or diagram needs one.

## Validation

From the repository root:

```sh
make source-check   # checkers, tests and numerical examples
make pdf            # latexmk -lualatex -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error compendium.tex
```

The checker validates entry structure, known citation keys and locators, duplicate labels, links from pathway notes, the Part and section declaration in `compendium.tex` against the guide, each chapter title against its guide section, each `\qfpathway` against the guide (pathway key, displayed name, parent section and relative order, at most once and with at least one typed entry; unwritten pathways may be omitted), that every topic input follows a pathway, so that no entry sits outside a numbered pathway, that wrappers have no other peer heading (a bare or starred `\section` or an unnumbered overview command), and that topic files hold no `\section`, `\chapter`, `\qfpathway` or overview command, an overview note for every populated section, bridge notes for retired guide overviews, heading levels, and that every non-empty topic file, including one with only headings or prose, is input exactly once, without cycles, through a wrapper's `\input` chain. Input paths are resolved first, so `a/topic` and `a/../a/topic` are the same file. Front matter in `front/` and the shared files at the top of `compendium/` are not topic files. `trading_notes.cli.check_coverage` validates the objective and outline-crosswalk records in `notes/coverage/`; a covered objective needs mapped entries, sources with locators, entry evidence and a review record. It does not establish the truth of a claim. After layout changes, inspect rendered pages containing chapter headings, lists, tables, equations and figures, and check the LaTeX log for overfull boxes and unresolved references.
