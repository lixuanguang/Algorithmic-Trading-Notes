\section{Curves and inventories}

\begin{qfdefinition}[Contango, backwardation and ending stocks: storage and financing costs, scarcity premia, seasonal crop curves, the stocks-to-use ratio, and roll yield stated carefully]\label{def:contango-backwardation-stocks}
\qffield{Statement} Gliner: commodities are driven by supply (producers, costs, yield, weather, politics) and demand (population, development, alternative energy) \citep[chap.~10, secs.\ ``Supply Drivers'' and ``Demand Drivers'', PDF pp.~293--296]{s7-gliner}. \emph{Ending stock} ``effectively measures the inventory of a particular commodity''; crops are harvested once or twice a year while demand is continuous, and the stock carried over becomes the next season's beginning balance (sec.\ ``Ending Stock'', p.~296, Figure~10.1). \emph{Contango} is near-term futures trading below later ones, from storage, transport and the opportunity cost of capital (gold in a vault); \emph{backwardation} is near-term futures at a premium, as in a supply shortage (cocoa during an Ivory Coast export stoppage) or just before a harvest when stocks are depleted; a crop curve can show both, contango after harvest and backwardation before the next (sec.\ ``Contango and Backwardation'', pp.~297--298, Figure~10.2, corn as of 30 September 2013).

\qffield{Notation} Balance sheet: beginning stocks $+$ production $+$ imports $-$ consumption $-$ exports $=$ ending stocks; stocks-to-use $=$ ending stocks $/$ total use. Full carry: $F=S e^{(r+u-y)T}$ with storage cost $u$ and convenience yield $y$ (\hyperref[res:cost-of-carry-forward-price]{cost-of-carry forward price}).

\qffield{Conditions} The legacy identity ``ending stocks $=$ beginning stocks $+$ production $-$ consumption'' holds for the world, not for a country, which also has trade; with imports of 5 and exports of 12 on a balance of 10, 100 and 95, ending stocks are 8, not 15 (\nolinkurl{notes/artefacts/multi-asset-systematic-macro/slice37-checks.py}, section~16). Low stocks relative to use support prices and make them sensitive to small supply changes, which is the storage model the pathway estimates; the legacy's ``most important driver of commodity prices'' is an overstatement of it.

\qffield{Practice} The monthly WASDE report \citep[catalogue entry only, not read]{physical-usda-wasde-2026} gives USDA's U.S.\ and world supply-and-demand balances. ``Normal state for storable commodities like gold'' (legacy contango) fits monetary metals with large above-ground stocks; it is not a general rule for storable commodities, which can be backwardated when inventories are scarce.

\qffield{Pitfalls} ``Contango creates negative roll yield; backwardation creates positive roll yield'' (legacy) is true of the roll component holding the curve fixed, not of total returns, and it says nothing on its own about a risk premium: whether backwardation reflects scarcity or compensation for risk is the pathway's question. \emph{Own bounded test} (batch of 2026-10-17, corrected rerun v2 the same day; not a reading, and not evidence beyond its sample). Data: EIA's public NYMEX series (contracts 1, 2 and~4, which EIA stopped updating on 5 April 2024) and EIA's weekly inventories. Backwardation is $B_t=\ln(C_{1,t}/C_{2,t})$ at complete month-ends. The inventory signal is the log deviation from the same week's mean over the previous five years, taken from the latest weekly report treated as public by settlement on $t$. The return is next month's return from holding contract~2, and each pair of month-ends is kept only if contract~2 at $t$ is contract~1 at $t+1$ under NYMEX's last-trading-day rules. The hypotheses and pass rules were written into the script before its first run, by the batch record's account. They were committed together with the results, so this is not a preregistration.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Scarcity: supported.} Backwardation rises as inventories fall. WTI, August 1987 to February 2024 (438 months): $\beta=-0.111$, Newey--West $t=-5.9$ ($C_1/C_4$: $t=-6.4$). Henry Hub, January 2015 to February 2024 (110 months): $\beta=-0.114$, $t=-4.3$. Both survive a two-week reporting lag.
\item \emph{Premium: not supported.} On the full price-only samples (WTI, January 1985 to February 2024, 470 months; Henry Hub, January 1994 to February 2024, 362 months), the slope of next-month returns on $B_t$ is $+0.02$ ($t=0.1$) for WTI and $-0.05$ ($t=-0.3$) for gas. Inventories add nothing. Out-of-sample $R^2$ against the historical mean is $-1.8$ percent for WTI (Clark--West $t=-0.4$) and, now computable, $-0.8$ percent for gas ($t=-1.4$, from January 2004).
\end{itemize}
Settlement-to-settlement figures are closing-price associations, since the signal and the entry use the same settlement. An \emph{executable variant} forms the signal at settlement~$t$ and trades the next day's settlement in the same contract, with two ticks per round trip (fills at settlement and the cost are assumptions; for Henry Hub, NYMEX's TAS order type covered the spot and the second through seventh months by May 2019, NYMEX Submission~19-158, but its availability in earlier years of the sample was not established). Exploratory only, added after the first run and not part of the test: in this variant WTI's long-backwardation, short-contango rule earned $+0.92$ percent a month (Newey--West $t=2.3$; $+1.91$ against $-0.16$ percent), while gas earned $+0.58$ ($t=0.9$). Two WTI and three gas months exceed $\pm50$ percent and are retained in every estimate.

\emph{What the v1 run (same day, kept in \nolinkurl{v1-2026-10-17/}) reported and why it is superseded:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Its last WTI and gas months paired 28 March 2024 with the incomplete month ending 5 April 2024, whose contract~1 was not 28 March's contract~2.
\item It used a fixed five-day inventory lag that admitted the gas report before its Thursday release.
\item It confined the gas premium test to the 111-month inventory sample, so no out-of-sample test was possible.
\item It presented settlement-timed rules without the execution caveat.
\item Its figures were WTI scarcity $\beta=-0.112$ ($t=-5.9$), premium slope $+0.05$ ($t=0.1$), out-of-sample $R^2$ $-2.2$ percent; gas scarcity $-0.124$ ($t=-4.4$), slope $-0.19$ ($t=-0.6$). Its exploratory WTI split was $+2.2$ against $-0.3$ percent ($t=3.4$), and its gas split $-5.0$ against $-0.4$; v2 gives $-1.3$ against $-0.9$ ($t=-0.2$).
\end{itemize}
Script, protocol, outputs and the derived monthly panel are \nolinkurl{notes/artefacts/commodity-storage-curves/backwardation_test.py}, \nolinkurl{backwardation_results.txt} and \nolinkurl{backwardation_monthly.csv}. \emph{A third commodity} (third batch of 2026-10-17). The same code, imported unchanged, was applied to NYMEX heating oil (EIA's ``New York Harbor No.~2 Heating Oil Future'', contracts 1 and~2) with U.S.\ distillate stocks (WDISTUS1, same Wednesday release as crude, so the crude lag of six days). The hypotheses, timing rules and acceptance criteria were written in \nolinkurl{batch60-heating-oil-protocol.md} before the data were downloaded, and committed with the results, so they are recorded in advance, not preregistered. The expiry rule assumed (last trading day of the month before delivery; rule text not obtained) is supported by the roll check in six of seven five-year blocks; the seventh, 2024, has one roll.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Scarcity: supported.} February 1994 to February 2024 (361 months), $\beta=-0.110$, $t=-5.3$, and $-0.104$ ($t=-5.3$) with a two-week lag.
\item \emph{Premium: not supported.} Slope $+0.36$, $t=1.67$, below the threshold of~2. Out-of-sample $R^2$ is $-0.9$ percent from February 2004, with Clark--West $t=0.37$.
\end{itemize}
The executable variant does not apply: heating oil expires at the month-end itself, so the contract that is $C_2$ at $t$ is already $C_1$ the next day. EIA's series keeps one label across the 2013 change from heating oil to ultra-low-sulphur diesel, so whether and how EIA spliced the two contracts was not established. Script and outputs: \nolinkurl{batch60_heating_oil_test.py}, \nolinkurl{batch60_heating_oil_results.txt} and \nolinkurl{batch60_heating_oil_monthly.csv}. Hedging-pressure readings, the other half of the backlog item, need positions data (for example the CFTC's Commitments of Traders), and were not tested. Limits: three U.S.\ energy contracts; a monthly horizon; no metals or agriculture. EIA's release tables cover only 2025--2026 \citep[holiday tables]{eia-weekly-release-schedules}, so availability rests on a conservative convention: six days after the week ending (crude), seven (gas), and eleven over Christmas and New Year. Henry Hub's pre-1997 expiry rule is inferred from the data, not from a rulebook.
