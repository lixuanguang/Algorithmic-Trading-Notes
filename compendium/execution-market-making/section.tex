\noindent The first section, from slice 35, covers what markets do, why people trade, zero-sum accounting and the limits of judging skill from profit, intermediaries, venues and market information systems, trading sessions and execution systems, and liquidity and market quality, for the overview \texttt{execution-market-making}, from Harris, Hasbrouck and Lehalle and Laruelle, with the London gold price dated from LBMA and IBA pages, and, from slice 36, dealers and their quotes and specialists' obligations and privileges, from Harris's 2002 account (none of the overview's readings yet studied). The next two sections, from slices 33 and 34, cover orders and their instructions, matching and trade pricing, the limit order book, limit orders as options, queues and imbalance measures (for the overview \texttt{execution-market-making}, \texttt{market-making-lob} and \texttt{lob-deep-learning}), and order flow in time, from stylised facts and realised variance to deseasonalisation, market clocks, point processes and Hawkes processes (for \texttt{market-simulation} and \texttt{market-making-lob}); they draw on Harris, Hasbrouck, Lehalle and Laruelle, Bouchaud et al., and two catalogue papers read in preprint, Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov and Bacry, Mastromatteo and Muzy, the latter a required reading of \texttt{market-making-lob}; none of these pathways' readings yet studied. Pathways drawn on: \texttt{market-making-lob} (draft entries from the migration: supplemental inventory, information and quoting models, Glosten--Milgrom, a practitioner account of dealer quoting, and textbook accounts of inventory-based quoting; of the pathway's required readings only Avellaneda--Stoikov and Glosten--Milgrom, through its 1983 working paper, are drawn on); and the section overview \texttt{execution-market-making} (draft entries from the migration, from Harris, Hasbrouck and Lehalle and Laruelle; none of its readings yet studied); and \texttt{hft-market-design} (draft entries from the migration: high-frequency trading as a set of strategies sharing speed tools, from the SEC's 2010 concept release and Lehalle and Laruelle; the latency-arbitrage arms race, from Budish, Cramton and Shim, a required reading checked in its published version; a stylised model in which a narrower spread need not lower other traders' costs, from Lehalle and Laruelle; and, from slice 36, the value of queue position, from Moallemi and Yuan's working paper, a reading of \texttt{market-making-lob} read in part; make/take fees and the speed race, from Lehalle and Laruelle's summary of Foucault, Kadan and Kandel; a toy model of the Flash Crash with its time term derived; and the Flash Crash of 6 May 2010, from the CFTC and SEC staff report; none of the pathway's readings yet studied). The section beginning with \hyperref[def:fundamental-value]{fundamental value} covers informed trading, informative prices, informed-trader types, the winner's curse, market efficiency, inside information and its deterrence, and the price formation process, from Harris and Lehalle and Laruelle; the section beginning with \hyperref[def:arbitrage]{arbitrage} covers arbitrageurs, order anticipators and bluffers, from Harris. The section beginning with \hyperref[def:spread-measures]{spread measures} measures and decomposes spreads, from Harris and Hasbrouck (the latter a required reading of this section's overview route, not yet studied); the section beginning with \hyperref[def:volatility-components]{fundamental and transitory volatility} covers volatility, returns by time scale, the relation of volatility to spreads, and bubbles and crashes, from Harris, Hasbrouck and Lehalle and Laruelle. A later section compares floor and automated trading systems, from Harris (2002). The last section, for \texttt{fx-dealer-market-making}, covers spot FX market structure, internalisation and last look, from the BIS Markets Committee's 2020 report, the FX Global Code, a provider disclosure and three of the pathway's readings; none of its readings yet studied. The section after it, for \texttt{options-execution}, covers option market-maker spreads, the measurement of option execution costs and retail option order routing, from six of the pathway's readings, one read in full and five through their abstracts; none yet studied.
