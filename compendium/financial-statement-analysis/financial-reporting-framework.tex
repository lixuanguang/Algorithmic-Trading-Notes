\subsection{Reporting framework and information sources}

These entries set out what statement analysis is for and how it proceeds, where the information comes from, and which reporting framework produced it. Standards are cited from their primary texts: IFRS as adopted in the EU and US GAAP through the FASB's Accounting Standards Updates and pre-Codification Statements. Effective dates are stated where a requirement has been issued but does not yet apply.

\begin{qfdefinition}[The statement-analysis framework: purpose-driven steps, the component analyses (business, accounting, financial, prospective) and the roles of analysis for credit, equity and other decisions]\label{def:statement-analysis-framework}
\qffield{Statement} Financial statement analysis applies analytical tools to general-purpose financial statements and related data to support estimates and inferences in business analysis: the evaluation of a company's prospects and risks for decisions such as equity and debt valuation, credit assessment, earnings prediction, audit testing and negotiations \citep[chap.~1, pp.~4--10]{financial-statement-analysis-2009}. It reduces reliance on intuition but does not replace judgement.

\emph{Roles.} Credit analysis asks whether a borrower can meet its obligations. It is asymmetric: the lender's upside is capped at interest, so variability of profit and cash flow under adverse conditions matters more than its level, through liquidity in the short term and solvency over the long term. Equity analysis is symmetric, assessing downside and upside, and usually ends in an estimate of intrinsic value compared with price. Managers, directors, acquirers, regulators, unions, suppliers and customers use the same tools for benchmarking, oversight and bargaining (pp.~7--10).

\emph{Components} (Exhibit~1.4, pp.~10--14):
\begin{itemize}
\item business environment and strategy analysis: industry structure (for example, competitive forces) and the firm's strategy;
\item accounting analysis: how far the accounts reflect economic reality, and the restatements needed for comparison;
\item financial analysis: profitability, risk, and the sources and uses of cash;
\item prospective analysis: forecasts of earnings and cash flows, which feed valuation with a cost of capital.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Steps} (pp.~636--639):
\begin{enumerate}
\item define the objective of the analysis;
\item formulate specific questions and decision criteria consistent with the user's risk and return requirements;
\item choose the tools that answer those questions;
\item interpret the evidence and reach a decision.
\end{enumerate}
A report separates evidence from inference. Its sections are an executive summary, an overview, the evidential matter, the assumptions, the crucial favourable and unfavourable factors, and the inferences.

\qffield{Notation} Building blocks: short-term liquidity; capital structure and solvency; return on invested capital; asset turnover; operating performance and profitability; forecasting and valuation (p.~638).

\qffield{Conditions} The steps describe a discipline, not a fixed checklist; their order and emphasis change with the decision. A short-term inventory loan needs liquidity, inventory turnover and cash forecasts, not long-run industry conditions (Illustrations CC.1a--d, pp.~636--637). The textbook's US regulatory and standards details date from 2008--2009; current requirements are in the entries that follow.

\qffield{Practice} \emph{Original application} (\hyperref[ex:integrated-credit-decision]{integrated credit decision}). A lender asked on 1~March 2026 to raise Harbour Tools' revolving facility from EUR~100 to 160 million follows the four steps. The objective is repayment capacity under stress, so the questions concern peak drawings, leverage and cover in a downside. The tools are a three-year statement forecast, ratio analysis and accounting adjustments for leases and pensions. The interpretation is a conditional approval. An equity analyst using the same statements would instead weight profitability, growth and the reinvestment needed to sustain them.

\qffield{Pitfalls} Computing every available ratio before deciding what question matters. Reading statements as reported without accounting analysis. Treating a credit analysis that finds adequate profitability as sufficient when variability under stress is the risk.

\qffield{See also} \hyperref[def:filings-disclosures-information-sources]{filings and sources}; \hyperref[meth:accounting-analysis-adjustments]{comparability adjustments}; \hyperref[ex:harbour-tools-statements]{Harbour case}.

\end{qfdefinition}

\begin{qfdefinition}[Filings, notes, management commentary, auditor's and control reports, and information beyond the financial statements]\label{def:filings-disclosures-information-sources}
\qffield{Statement} \emph{The financial statements.} Under IFRS a complete set comprises a statement of financial position, a statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, a statement of changes in equity, a statement of cash flows, notes with material accounting policy information and comparatives. An opening balance sheet of the earliest period is added after a retrospective change \citep[IAS~1 para.~10]{eu-2023-1803-ias1}. The notes disclose the judgements with the most significant effect and the sources of estimation uncertainty that carry a significant risk of material adjustment within a year (paras.~122 and~125). They also hold commitments and contingencies, business combinations, related-party transactions, share-option plans, segment data and legal proceedings \citep[pp.~27--28]{financial-statement-analysis-2009}.

\emph{Regulatory filings.} In the United States the annual report on Form~10-K, the quarterly Form~10-Q, current reports on Form~8-K for events such as an auditor change, proxy statements on directors' pay and ownership, and prospectuses carry the statements and much of the context (Exhibit~2.1, p.~70). Management's discussion and analysis must provide material information for assessing financial condition and results, including the amounts and certainty of cash flows. It must explain known events and uncertainties reasonably likely to make reported results unrepresentative of the future, analyse liquidity over the next twelve months and beyond, and explain material changes in line items, including offsetting changes \citep[para.~(a)--(b)]{sec-sk-item-303}. Risk factors must be material, specific and grouped under headings, with generic risks at the end \citep[paras.~(a)--(b)]{sec-sk-item-105}.

\emph{Assurance and controls.} For EU public-interest entities, the auditor's report must state the auditor's appointment and uninterrupted tenure, the most significant assessed risks of material misstatement (including fraud), the response and key observations, and how far the audit could detect irregularities \citep[Art.~10(2)]{eu-audit-regulation-2014}. A US registrant's management reports on internal control over financial reporting. It cannot call controls effective if there is a material weakness, and accelerated filers add the auditor's attestation \citep[para.~(a)]{sec-sk-item-308}. Anything less than an unqualified audit opinion raises the risk of the analysis \citep[p.~27]{financial-statement-analysis-2009}.

\emph{Beyond the reports} (pp.~68--69, 74--75): earnings announcements, which usually precede the audited statements; voluntary disclosure, motivated by liability, expectation adjustment, signalling or expectation management; economy, industry and company news; and information intermediaries, such as sell-side and buy-side analysts and rating agencies, who gather, interpret, forecast and recommend.

\qffield{Notation} Not applicable.

\qffield{Conditions} The SEC items are cited from the current text of 17~CFR as displayed by the Legal Information Institute, retrieved 20~October 2026. The EU audit-report content applies to statutory audits of public-interest entities; Member States may add requirements (Art.~10(2)). US critical-audit-matter requirements under PCAOB standards were not consulted, because the PCAOB site refused access; the EU article is cited instead. Quarterly data need care because of seasonality and year-end adjustments (p.~68).

\qffield{Practice} \emph{Interpretation.} For Harbour Tools (\hyperref[ex:harbour-tools-statements]{case}), the analyst's questions map to sources as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item the 2025 restructuring charge to the provisions note and management commentary;
\item the revolver drawing at year-end to the borrowings note and the liquidity discussion;
\item the pension deficit to the IAS~19 sensitivity and expected-contribution disclosures (\hyperref[meth:post-employment-benefit-modelling]{pension modelling});
\item the auditor's significant risks to the auditor's report;
\item demand and price conditions to industry data and competitors' filings.
\end{itemize}
A headline ``adjusted'' profit in a press release is checked against the reconciliation that \hyperref[meth:earnings-management-presentation]{non-GAAP rules} require.

\qffield{Pitfalls} Relying on the earnings release without the notes. Treating management commentary as neutral. Assuming the auditor's report guarantees accuracy; it expresses reasonable assurance on the statements taken as a whole.

\qffield{See also} \hyperref[def:statement-analysis-framework]{framework}; \hyperref[meth:cash-flow-balance-sheet-quality]{risk information}; \hyperref[def:reporting-incentives-discipline]{discipline mechanisms}.

\end{qfdefinition}

\begin{qfdefinition}[Alternative reporting systems: IFRS and US GAAP, how standards become applicable, their main differences for analysis, and monitoring issued but not yet effective requirements]\label{def:ifrs-us-gaap-reporting-systems}
\qffield{Statement} \emph{Who sets and who enforces.} IFRS are issued by the International Accounting Standards Board. In the European Union, publicly traded companies have prepared consolidated accounts under international standards adopted by Commission Regulation for financial years starting on or after 1~January 2005. The adopted text is the one applicable in the EU \citep[Regulation (EU) 2023/1803, recitals~1--2]{eu-2023-1803-ias1}, and each amending regulation sets the latest date from which companies must apply an amendment, for example financial years starting on or after 1~January 2024 for the IAS~1 covenant amendments \citep[Art.~2]{eu-2023-2822-ias1}. US GAAP is set by the Financial Accounting Standards Board in a political process involving preparers and users, and the SEC is the final authority for registrants \citep[pp.~70--72]{financial-statement-analysis-2009}. Since 2008 the SEC has accepted financial statements of foreign private issuers prepared under IFRS as issued by the IASB without reconciliation to US GAAP, for financial years ending after 15~November 2007 \citep[p.~986]{sec-ifrs-acceptance-2008}.

\emph{Differences that change analysis} (cited in the entries that follow):
\begin{itemize}
\item inventory: LIFO is permitted under US GAAP but not IFRS, and IAS~2 reverses earlier write-downs (\hyperref[meth:inventory-cost-formulas]{inventory});
\item development costs meeting six criteria are capitalised under IAS~38 but expensed under US GAAP outside software (\hyperref[meth:intangible-acquisition-routes]{intangibles});
\item impairment: IAS~36 tests recoverable amount and reverses losses other than on goodwill; US GAAP first tests recoverability on undiscounted cash flows and prohibits reversal (\hyperref[meth:impairment-derecognition]{impairment});
\item leases: a single lessee model under IFRS~16 against finance and operating leases under Topic~842 (\hyperref[meth:lease-reporting]{leases});
\item pensions: IAS~19 net interest and remeasurements in OCI against an expected return on assets with deferred amortisation under US GAAP (\hyperref[meth:post-employment-benefit-reporting]{pensions});
\item cash-flow classification of interest and dividends (\hyperref[meth:statement-linked-cash-flows]{cash flows}).
\end{itemize}

\emph{Monitoring developments.} An issued requirement can change reported numbers before the economics change:
\begin{itemize}
\item IFRS~18 replaces IAS~1 for annual periods beginning on or after 1~January 2027, retrospectively, with a reconciliation of each comparative income-statement line \citep[Annex, IFRS~18 paras.~C1--C3 and~C8]{eu-2026-338-ifrs18};
\item US expense disaggregation applies to public business entities' annual periods beginning after 15~December 2026 \citep[p.~3]{us-expense-disaggregation-2024}; non-calendar year-ends first adopt in an annual period \citep[p.~1]{us-expense-disaggregation-date-2025};
\item income-tax disclosure changes apply to public business entities from annual periods beginning after 15~December 2024 \citep[p.~5]{us-tax-disclosures-2023};
\item internal-use software rules change for annual periods beginning after 15~December 2027 \citep[p.~3]{us-software-2025}.
\end{itemize}

\qffield{Notation} Issued: published by the standard-setter. Adopted: given legal force in a jurisdiction (EU endorsement). Effective: mandatory for the reporting period in question. Early application can make two companies' statements for the same period follow different requirements.

\qffield{Conditions} As of 20~October 2026. Each difference above is stated in the cited entry with its source and date; this entry does not list every difference. The textbook's description of IFRS as not applicable in the United States, with reconciliation required, predates the 2008 rule and is superseded by it. The FASB Codification itself was not consulted; US paragraphs are cited from the Updates and Statements that issued them, so later amendments not in those documents are not reflected.

\qffield{Practice} \emph{Original.} Harbour (IFRS) and a US GAAP peer, Ridgeline, report EBIT margins of 8.61 and 8.75 percent for 2025, and net debt to EBITDA of 1.63 and 1.03 times. Several adjustments put both on a common basis:
\begin{itemize}
\item Ridgeline's LIFO inventory is restated to FIFO;
\item its operating-lease cost is replaced by right-of-use depreciation and lease interest;
\item Harbour's capitalised development is expensed.
\end{itemize}
Ridgeline's margin lead then widens to 2.31 points, and the leverage gap narrows from 0.59 to 0.36 times (\hyperref[meth:accounting-analysis-adjustments]{comparability adjustments}). The frameworks, not the businesses, explained most of the apparent margin similarity.

\qffield{Pitfalls} Comparing ratios across frameworks without adjustment. Treating EU-adopted IFRS and IASB IFRS as identical without checking endorsement dates. Ignoring an issued standard because it is not yet effective when forecasting statements that will be prepared under it.

\qffield{See also} \hyperref[meth:accounting-change-effects]{effects of accounting changes}; \hyperref[meth:statement-linked-cash-flows]{cash-flow classification}.

\end{qfdefinition}
