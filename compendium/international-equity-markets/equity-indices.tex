\section{Equity indices and instruments for macro}

\begin{qfdefinition}[Equity indices and instruments for macro: major indices by region, price, capitalisation and equal weighting, and ETFs, ADRs, index futures and options as ways to trade them]\label{def:equity-indices-derivatives}
\qffield{Statement} Gliner's Table~8.1 lists major indices by region: United States (S\&P 500, Nasdaq, DJIA, Russell 2000, Wilshire 5000), Europe (Euro Stoxx 50, DAX, CAC 40, FTSE 100, FTSE MIB, IBEX 35, AEX), Asia ex-Japan (Shanghai Composite, Hang Seng, KOSPI, ASX 200, NZX 50), Japan (Nikkei, Topix, JASDAQ) and emerging markets (Bovespa, NIFTY, Micex and RTS, Mexico IPC, TOP 40 and JALSH, BIST 30 and 100, Jakarta Composite); indices ``can be traded directionally or as a relative value trade'', and their exposures differ, so ``the Bovespa is more likely to be affected by commodities than the NIFTY, while the S\&P 500 has more financial exposure than the Nasdaq, which is more technology-weighted'' \citep[chap.~8, sec.\ ``Equity Indices Overview'', PDF pp.~214--215]{s7-gliner}. Price-weighted indices consider only prices and ``can be strongly affected by the move of one'' stock; capitalisation-weighted indices are dominated by the largest companies; ``it is useful to know the weighting structure of each index in order to accurately interpret its meaning'' (pp.~214--215). Instruments (sec.\ ``Equity Derivatives'', PDF pp.~218--221): ETFs, ``a fast, cheap way to get exposures'' that trade like stocks but charge fees, whose composition can differ from the index (EWZ held as much as 15 percent Petrobras, more than the Bovespa); ADRs, a depositary bank's holdings of a non-U.S.\ stock listed in the United States in dollars, ``subject to foreign exchange risk''.

\qffield{Notation} Price-weighted: $I_t\propto\sum_i P_{i,t}$; capitalisation-weighted: $I_t\propto\sum_i N_{i,t}P_{i,t}$ (often free-float adjusted); equal-weighted: equal weights reset at each rebalance.

\qffield{Conditions} Index lists, constituents and ETF holdings are as of 2013. \emph{Two changes checked} (second slice-37 follow-up). JASDAQ is no longer a market segment: ``On April 4, 2022, TSE reorganized the former market segments (First Section, Second Section, Mothers and JASDAQ) into three new market segments: Prime, Standard and Growth'' \citep[market-structure page, retrieved 2026-10-16]{jpx-market-restructure}, so a JASDAQ index in Gliner's sense describes a market that no longer exists. Moscow Exchange now publishes the ``MOEX Russia Index and RTS Index'', listing ``the MICEX Index'' only as a registered trademark \citep[index page, retrieved 2026-10-16]{moex-indices}; Gliner's ``Micex'' is the MOEX Russia Index (the date of the renaming was not given on the page). \emph{Two weighting schemes checked} (source follow-up, 2026-10-16). The Nikkei 225 ``is a price-weighted equity index, which consists of 225 stocks in the Prime Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange'', reviewed semi-annually and computed every five seconds \citep[profile page, retrieved 2026-10-16]{nikkei-225-profile}; TOPIX ``is a free-float adjusted market capitalization-weighted index'', with base value 100 on 4 January 1968 \citep[TOPIX page, retrieved 2026-10-16]{jpx-topix}. So Japan's two headline indices sit on opposite sides of the price-versus-capitalisation distinction above. \emph{Two more checked} (second source follow-up, 2026-10-16). South Africa: Gliner's TOP 40 and JALSH are the FTSE/JSE Top 40 and All Share indices, two of seven ``headline'' indices in FTSE Russell's FTSE/JSE Africa series; the Top 40 is ``the 40 most investable companies ranked by investable market capitalisation, in the FTSE/JSE All Share Index'', and the headline indices ``were harmonised effective 18 March 2024'' to reflect the SWIX free float, the shareholder-weighted method of the rules' Appendix~C \citep[v9.3, September 2026, secs.~1.7.1 and~4.5.1, pp.~4 and~13]{ftse-jse-ground-rules-2026}. United States: the Wilshire 5000 is now the FT Wilshire 5000, ``a comprehensive, float adjusted measure of the U.S. stock market, designed to reflect the performance of all publicly traded U.S. equity securities that have readily available prices'', with capitalisation-based size segments \citep[WI-METH-001, January 2026, sec.~1.1, p.~4]{ft-wilshire-5000-2026}; its name is not a constituent count. \emph{Two more} (batch of 2026-10-17). India: ``The Nifty 50 is a 50 stock, float-adjusted market-capitalization weighted index for India'', its stocks representing ``about 66\%'' of NSE's float-adjusted capitalisation \citep[Methodology Document for Equity Indices, September 2026, PDF p.~19]{nse-nifty-methodology-2026} (the same page's turnover sentence also says ``float-adjusted market capitalization'', apparently a slip). Turkey: BIST indices weight by free-float market value, with a weighting factor below one only in capped versions; BIST~100 ``automatically covers BIST 30 and BIST 50 stocks'', and the capped variants bound weights at 10 or 25 percent with thresholds of 15 or 30 percent \citep[Ground Rules, April 2020, secs.~2.2--2.3, 2.20 and~3.1, pp.~1--5]{bist-ground-rules-2020}, the edition served at Borsa Istanbul's address on 2026-10-17. \qffield{Practice} For relative-value index trades, size legs by beta and volatility, and hedge or deliberately keep the currency exposure; for the section's outcome, compare markets in local currency and in dollars, hedged and unhedged.

\qffield{Pitfalls} An ADR's dollar price follows the local share price converted at the exchange rate, so it carries currency risk for a dollar investor, not because ``shares are held locally'' but because the underlying is priced in local currency. The legacy's index futures (``liquid, leveraged exposure'') and options (``asymmetric payoffs'') are generic; futures conventions are in \hyperref[conv:cme-globex-trading]{CME Globex trading} and ETF mechanics in \hyperref[mech:etf-creation-redemption]{ETF creation and redemption}.
