\noindent Pathways drawn on: the section overview \texttt{multi-asset-systematic-macro} (four draft entries from the migration: what global macro is and how its approaches differ, its return record in crises, position sizing and risk limits, and practitioner frameworks from Drobny's interviews), \texttt{macro-regimes-stock-bond} (a map of how asset classes and sectors are said to respond to growth, recession, inflation and liquidity shocks), \texttt{macro-nowcasting-signals} (the macroeconomic indicators and the equity leading indicators a macro trader watches), \texttt{macro-announcements-policy} (the U.S.\ release calendar and event queries around releases) and \texttt{cross-asset-style-premia} (equity-index valuation ratios). The entries came from the legacy macro chapter, written from Gliner's \emph{Global Macro Trading} (Wiley, 2014) and Drobny's \emph{Inside the House of Money} (Wiley, 2006 printing) and checked against them and against dated primary sources in slice~37; none of these pathways' readings has been studied, and the entries are descriptions of practice, not tested results. The legacy chapter's opening claim, that global macro identifies regime shifts early and that its primary objective is to survive and profit from crises rather than to maximise returns in stable periods, is Gliner's description of the strategy's aim (capital preservation), restated in \hyperref[def:global-macro-strategy]{global macro}; the rest of that chapter went to the rates, money-market, currency, credit, equity, volatility and commodity chapters, listed in \texttt{notes/migration-slices.md}, slice~37.
