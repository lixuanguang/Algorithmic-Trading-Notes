\section{Natural gas}

\begin{qfdefinition}[Natural gas for macro traders: producers, shale, flaring, LNG and the Henry Hub futures contract as Gliner describes them in 2013]\label{def:natural-gas-market}
\qffield{Statement} Gliner calls natural gas ``the cleanest and safest fossil fuel'', producing ``30 to 50 percent less carbon dioxide than gasoline or coal''; it is used in power generation, heating, transport and to make ammonia for fertiliser \citep[chap.~10, sec.\ ``Natural Gas'', PDF pp.~313--316]{s7-gliner}. Shale gas, produced with horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing, is expected to supply 50 percent of U.S.\ gas by 2020 (p.~314). Flaring burns unwanted gas, abundant in the United States where it is produced alongside oil even when there is no demand (p.~315). Cooled to $-260^\circ$F, gas becomes LNG, taking ``600 times less space'' (p.~315). The United States and Russia account for 38 percent of world production (Table~10.5, p.~316). Gas is priced in dollars per million British thermal units (MMBtu), roughly one thousand cubic feet (p.~317); the NYMEX contract (NG) is for delivery at Henry Hub, Louisiana; the front of the curve is ``predominantly a speculators' market'' and the back a producers' hedging market; the UNG ETF tracks the contract, with contango and rolls a caution (pp.~317--318).

\qffield{Notation} MMBtu million British thermal units; Mcf thousand cubic feet; LNG liquefied natural gas.

\qffield{Conditions} Figures are Gliner's for 2012--2013. Emissions comparisons depend on whether methane leakage is counted. Gliner gives the contract as ``10,000 million British thermal units'' (10,000 MMBtu, p.~317), as of 2013, which the current chapter confirms (below). \emph{Delivery, as amended in 2016} (source follow-up, 2026-10-16). NYMEX Rule~220104 of the Henry Hub Natural Gas futures (NG), as certified to the CFTC on 11 July 2016 with effect from 26 July 2016, provides that ``Delivery shall be made free-on-board (`F.O.B.') at the buyer's interconnection point at the Henry Hub'', is complete when gas passes that point ``via physical flow or displacement'', and recognises the Intra Hub Transfer service as an acceptable interconnection point; the filing describes the hub as operated by Sabine Pipe Line \citep[Submission 16-080, pp.~1--3 and Appendix~A]{nymex-16-080}. \emph{Current chapter} (official PDF downloaded through a browser on 2026-10-16; no amendment date in the text, and its page footer reads ``\copyright\ Copyright 2009''). The trading unit is 10{,}000 MMBtu with a delivery tolerance of 2 percent either way; the minimum fluctuation is \$0.001 per MMBtu (\$10 a contract), with \$0.00025 for inter-commodity spreads on Globex; trading ends on the third business day before the first day of the delivery month; and Rule~220104 reads as certified in 2016, with Sabine Pipe Line LLC as the hub operator \citep[Rules 220101--220104]{nymex-ch220-2026}. Whether Chapter~220 was amended after 2016 in respects not covered here cannot be told from the undated text.

\qffield{Practice} Henry Hub is one hub; regional hubs and LNG-linked prices can diverge from it, which is the pathway's subject.

\qffield{Pitfalls} The legacy said flaring is ``common in US due to regulatory requirements''; Gliner attributes it to gas produced where there is no demand or infrastructure. ``Shale revolution \dots\ transformed US from importer to exporter'' (legacy) goes beyond Gliner's 2013 account and was not checked.
