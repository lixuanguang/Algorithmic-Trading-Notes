\subsection{The fixed-income universe}

\begin{qfdefinition}[The fixed-income universe for macro: money markets, government, inflation-linked, corporate, municipal, mortgage and asset-backed bonds, and fixed-income ETFs]\label{def:fixed-income-universe}
\qffield{Statement} Gliner opens his fixed-income chapter with bank credit and debt capital markets funding governments, corporations and consumers, and covers money markets, LIBOR, swaps, fed funds, OIS, FRAs, futures, TIPS, sovereign credit and CDS \citep[chap.~9, opening, PDF p.~245]{s7-gliner}; he calls fixed income ``the most important'' product group for macro (chap.~4, PDF p.~95). The fixed-income universe comprises money markets (maturities under a year: bills, commercial paper, repo, fed funds, SOFR); government bonds; inflation-linked bonds (TIPS, U.K.\ index-linked gilts, French OATi); corporate bonds, investment grade and high yield; municipal bonds, often tax-exempt; agency and non-agency MBS, with prepayment risk; and ABS. Gliner's ETF list (sec.\ ``Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs)'', PDF p.~280) gives liquid access to these segments.

\qffield{Notation} IG and HY the investment-grade and high-yield credit classes; agency MBS guaranteed by GSEs.

\qffield{Conditions} Whether fixed income is the largest of the four product groups by notional outstanding is an ill-posed question: SIFMA's highlights give global fixed-income debt outstanding of \$160.7 trillion and global equity market capitalisation of \$157.8 trillion for 2025 \citep[Fact Book highlights, sec.~1, published 13 August 2026]{sifma-factbook-2026}, similar magnitudes measured differently (face or market value against market value), while FX and commodities are traded mainly through derivatives, whose notionals (\$579 trillion for interest rate derivatives alone at end-June 2024, \hyperref[def:interest-rate-swaps]{interest-rate swaps}) are not comparable with stocks of securities.

\qffield{Practice} Gliner's Table~9.5 lists SHY, IEF, TLT, TBT, AGG, LQD, TIP, MBB, MUB, PCY, HYG, JNK and BKLN (p.~280); EMB is an additional example. These are funds as of 2013; check each fund's current prospectus before use. Inverse and leveraged ETFs such as TBT reset daily, so their multi-day returns are not the stated multiple of the index's.

\qffield{Pitfalls} An ETF's price can deviate from the value of its bonds in stress; creation and redemption arbitrage is in \hyperref[mech:etf-creation-redemption]{ETF creation and redemption}.

\qffield{See also} \hyperref[def:money-market-reference-rates]{money markets}; \hyperref[def:tips-breakevens]{TIPS}; \hyperref[def:sovereign-credit-defaults]{sovereign credit}; \hyperref[mech:cds-contract-credit-events]{CDS}; \hyperref[def:fixed-income-market-structure]{fixed-income market structure}.

\qffield{Further reading} The section's readings on each segment.

\end{qfdefinition}
