\section{The research and production chain of a strategy}

\begin{qfmethod}[Strategy production lifecycle: López de Prado's research stations and the embargo, paper-trading, graduation, re-allocation and decommission stages]\label{meth:strategy-production-lifecycle}
\qffield{Statement} López de Prado argues that a firm, not a lone researcher, should produce machine-learning strategies, under what he calls the meta-strategy paradigm: ``It takes almost as much effort to produce one true investment strategy as to produce a hundred'', because of data curation, high-performance computing, software, feature analysis, execution simulators and backtesting \citep[chap.~1, sec.~1.2.2, p.~5]{alpha-afml}. He describes the stations of the production chain (sec.~1.3.1, pp.~6--8):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{data curators} collect, clean, index, store, adjust and deliver all data, as experts in market microstructure and data protocols such as FIX;
\item \emph{feature analysts} turn raw data into informative signals and catalogue findings, but do not develop strategies;
\item \emph{strategists} turn features into strategies, each strategy being ``the experiment designed to test the validity'' of a theory that must identify ``the economic mechanism that causes an agent to lose money to us''; ``features may be discovered by a black box, but the strategy is developed in a white box'';
\item \emph{backtesters} assess the strategy under several scenarios and evaluate ``the probability of backtest overfitting by taking into account the number of trials''; their results go to management and are not shared;
\item the \emph{deployment team} integrates the code into production, keeps it ``logically identical to the prototype'' and optimises it for latency;
\item \emph{portfolio oversight}, which follows a deployed strategy through five stages (pp.~8--9):
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{embargo}: running on data after the backtest's end date; promotion if performance is consistent with the backtest;
  \item \emph{paper trading} on a live feed, capturing data, calculation and execution delays, ``for as long as it is needed to gather enough evidence that the strategy performs as expected'';
  \item \emph{graduation}: managing a real position, alone or in an ensemble, with risk, returns and costs attributed;
  \item \emph{re-allocation}: allocations reassessed ``frequently and automatically'' in a diversified portfolio, ``in general'' following a concave function, small at graduation, rising while the strategy performs and shrinking as performance decays;
  \item \emph{decommission}, once performance has lagged long enough ``to conclude that the supporting theory is no longer backed by empirical evidence''.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}
He prefers releasing new variations to run in parallel with old ones (p.~9).

\qffield{Conditions} This is one practitioner's prescription as of 2018, presented as what ``every successful quantitative firm I am aware of'' does (p.~5), not a regulatory standard or a tested result. None of the stages carries a quantitative criterion: how much consistency promotes a strategy from embargo, how long paper trading lasts, the shape of the concave allocation function and the length of underperformance that triggers decommission are all left to the firm. His view that ``the only true alpha left is microscopic'' and needs ``heavy ML tools'', while the chances of an individual finding macroscopic alpha with simple tools such as econometrics are ``quickly converging to zero'' (p.~6), is an opinion without data in the passage.

\qffield{Practice} Interpretation: the stages map onto model-governance controls. Embargo and paper trading come after deployment in López de Prado's sequence (pp.~8--9), so they are validation before live-capital trading, that is before graduation, not before production; graduation, re-allocation and decommission are change and approval decisions that need an owner, evidence and a record (\hyperref[meth:quant-strategy-monitoring]{strategy monitoring} supplies the evidence; \hyperref[meth:automated-trading-operational-controls]{operational controls} the pre-trade and kill-switch layer). The backtester's count of trials is the subject of \hyperref[pit:parameter-overfitting]{parameter overfitting} and \hyperref[meth:backtest-evaluation-diagnostics]{backtest diagnostics}. An embargo period is one further sample, of whatever length the backtest left out; a promotion decided on it is another trial, and its strength depends on that length, which the source does not set.

\qffield{Pitfalls} Decommissioning follows a loss of empirical support for the theory; the proposed concave allocation holds only ``in general''. The organisational framework is a proposal, not evidence of effectiveness. The alpha-models file's machine-learning item, on the cost of producing strategies and macroscopic against microscopic alpha, is the same author's sec.~1.2.2 and~1.3.1 (pp.~5--6), not Narang's; it is represented here.

\qffield{See also} \hyperref[meth:hypothesis-driven-strategy-research]{hypothesis-driven strategy research} (theory before testing, in Narang's version); \hyperref[def:quant-strategy-components]{components of a quant strategy}; \hyperref[def:quant-strategy-risk-sources]{sources of strategy risk} (implementation errors in deployment).

\end{qfmethod}

\begin{qfmethod}[Promoting, re-allocating and decommissioning a strategy model: owners, records and evidence from supervisory model risk guidance, against López de Prado's unquantified stages]\label{meth:strategy-promotion-criteria}
\qffield{Statement} Two current supervisory texts set out who decides that a model may be used, on what evidence and with what record. Both are written for banks.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{United Kingdom.} The PRA's SS1/23 (May 2023) applies to banks and PRA-designated investment firms with internal-model approval (paras.~1.2--1.3) \citep[para.~1.2, PDF p.~3]{wide-pra-ss123}. Development testing ``should demonstrate that a model works as intended'', with ``clear criteria (tests)'' to measure quality and choose between candidates. Developers set out the ``monitoring pack'' of tests for the model's use (Principle~3.3(a), PDF p.~21). A material change, including drift in models that recalibrate themselves, is tested by comparing output before and after the change with actual outcomes (Principle~3.3(c), p.~22). An independent validation function reviews and periodically re-validates models and recommends approvals ``to the appropriate model approval authority'' (Principle~4.1(b), p.~25).

Validators ``should therefore not be part of any model development activities, nor have a stake in whether or not a model is approved'' (para.~2.14, PDF p.~8). The model-risk control functions' authority includes the power ``to restrict the use of models, recommend conditional approval, or temporarily grant exceptions to model validation or approval'' (para.~2.13, pp.~7--8). Firm policy names the approval authorities and the procedure for material changes (Principle~2.3(c)(viii), p.~17). The board's model risk appetite covers approving models for decision-making, limits on use and exceptions, and ``thresholds for acceptable model performance'' (p.~14). The rationale for \emph{decommissioning} a model is recorded and can inform later development (n.~6, p.~11).
\item \emph{United States.} SR~26-2 of 17 April 2026, issued with the OCC and FDIC, ``supersedes and replaces'' SR~11-7 \citep[cover letter]{sr-26-2-2026}. Its revised guidance asks for monitoring procedures ``before and after a model is approved for use''. Outcomes analysis, ``back-testing or outlier analysis'' among its forms, is reviewed in validation. A model that ``no longer performs as expected may warrant overlays, adjustment, or redevelopment'' (secs.~IV--V, PDF pp.~7 and~9--10). ``Effective challenge'' requires expertise, independence and the standing ``to effect any change'' (p.~5).
\end{itemize}

\qffield{Conditions} Guidance as of 17 April 2026 (U.S.) and May 2023, effective 17 May 2024 (UK). Interpretation: neither text sets numerical promotion thresholds. Both leave the criteria and tolerances to the firm's documented risk appetite and policy, so a strategy's promotion rule (minimum embargo length, paper-trading tracking error, capacity) is the firm's to set and record in advance. Applying bank model-risk guidance to a trading strategy is by analogy: a strategy is a model in the guidance's sense only when its outputs inform decisions under the firm's model definition.

\qffield{Practice} Mapping onto \hyperref[meth:strategy-production-lifecycle]{López de Prado's stages} (interpretation). \emph{Embargo and paper trading} are development and pre-approval testing against criteria and a monitoring pack fixed beforehand. \emph{Graduation} is approval by a named authority on an independent validation recommendation, with any conditions recorded. \emph{Re-allocation} is a material change, validated by comparing before and after against outcomes. \emph{Decommissioning} is recorded with its reason. The record for each step: the criteria and tests set beforehand, the results, the validator's opinion, the approver and conditions, and the monitoring pack. Pre-specified criteria also protect against \hyperref[pit:parameter-overfitting]{overfitting} the promotion rule itself.

\qffield{Pitfalls} SS1/23's scope is UK banks with internal-model approval (para.~1.2), and SR~26-2 is ``most relevant'' to banks above \$30 billion in assets (cover letter). Neither binds an asset manager or proprietary trading firm as such; they are used here as the most specific public statements of practice. SR~11-7 (2011), which the catalogue still lists, is superseded.

\qffield{See also} \hyperref[meth:strategy-production-lifecycle]{strategy production lifecycle}; \hyperref[meth:discretionary-override-governance]{discretionary overrides}; \hyperref[meth:quant-strategy-monitoring]{strategy monitoring}; \hyperref[rule:mifid-rts6-algorithmic-controls]{RTS~6} (EU algorithm testing and deployment).

\qffield{Further reading} \nolinkurl{w4-simonian-model-governance}; SR~26-2's attachment in full.

\end{qfmethod}

\begin{qfmethod}[Who may override a model, on what evidence, with what record and review: post-model adjustments and expert judgement under supervisory guidance, against Narang's discretionary overrides]\label{meth:discretionary-override-governance}
\qffield{Statement} SS1/23 treats adjustments to a model's input, assumptions, methodology or output by expert judgement as part of the model's governance \citep[Principles~3.4 and~5.1, PDF pp.~22--23 and~28]{wide-pra-ss123}.
\begin{itemize}
\item Firms must show that risks from model limitations and uncertainty are understood, monitored and managed, ``including through the use of expert judgement'' (Principle~3.4(a)).
\item A high materiality of adjustments, or recurring adjustments for the same limitation, indicates ``flawed model design or misspecification'' and calls for recalibration or redevelopment (Principle~3.4, p.~23).
\item Post-model adjustments (PMAs) follow ``a consistent firm-wide process'', documented in policy, with a governance and control framework covering their use, calculation, completeness, ``and when PMAs should be reduced or removed'' (Principle~5.1(a)). There must be ``a clear rationale for the use of PMAs to compensate for model limitations'' (5.1(b)). PMAs for material models ``should be documented, supported by senior management, and approved by the appropriate level of authority (eg senior management, risk committee, audit committee)'' (5.1(c)).
\end{itemize}
SR~26-2 likewise lets monitoring lead to ``overlays, adjustment, or redevelopment'' under the firm's model risk management policy \citep[sec.~V, PDF p.~9]{sr-26-2-2026}.

\qffield{Conditions} Interpretation, against \hyperref[def:quant-strategy-risk-sources]{Narang's discretionary overrides}. An override of a trading model is an adjustment to its output. Under these texts it needs a rationale tied to a stated limitation, an authority fixed in advance, a record, and a review that decides when it ends. A pre-approved contingency rule (a de-risking trigger, a kill switch) meets that before the event; an improvised override must meet it after, and recurring ones point to redesign. Neither text says when overriding is right. The evidence Narang offers from August 2007 is one episode, in which discretionary unwinding spread losses.

\qffield{Practice} A minimal override record (interpretation): time, model and positions affected; the stated limitation or event; who authorised it, against a policy authority; the expected effect and the exit condition; and an after-the-fact review comparing the overridden and model outputs with outcomes. That is SS1/23's parallel outcomes analysis applied to one decision.

\qffield{Pitfalls} Scope as in \hyperref[meth:strategy-promotion-criteria]{strategy promotion}: bank guidance applied by analogy.

\qffield{See also} \hyperref[def:quant-strategy-risk-sources]{sources of risk in a quant strategy}; \hyperref[meth:strategy-promotion-criteria]{strategy promotion}; \hyperref[meth:automated-trading-operational-controls]{operational controls} (kill functionality).

\qffield{Further reading} \nolinkurl{w4-simonian-model-governance}.

\end{qfmethod}
