## Key results

- sec. 2: r = mu + beta f + e with E[f] = 0 and E[e] = 0; approximate APT (mu - X lambda)' Z^-1 (mu - X lambda) <= a; exact pricing mu = i lambda_0 + beta lambda_1 when a = 0.
- sec. 3: Arrow-Debreu intuition; the residual term makes the link to exact pricing tenuous; proofs add a law of large numbers.
- sec. 4: Huberman's definition of arbitrage (zero-cost portfolios whose expected income grows without bound and whose variance vanishes); diagonal, bounded residual covariance gives the approximate bound; exact pricing in the limit for well-diversified portfolios; Ingersoll, Chamberlain and Rothschild.
- sec. 6: exact APT as mean-variance efficiency of factor portfolios; any frontier portfolio can serve as a "factor"; a test is a joint test of the factors and the pricing relation. sec. 7: OLS factor regression, eqs. (14)-(18); cross-sectional regressions, eqs. (24)-(25).
- sec. 8: three factor-identification approaches (algorithmic, judgement guided by the covariance matrix, pure judgement); the third is in the spirit of the ICAPM; the number of factors is "exploding".
- sec. 9: asset allocation (k assets span the frontier), cost of capital (utility rate cases; the commission used the CAPM) and performance evaluation; the APT concerns static portfolios, so its use for managed portfolios contradicts its no-arbitrage spirit.
