## Key results

- pp. 26-28, Fig. 1: minimum-variance frontier abc for risky assets; only portfolios above b efficient; lending/borrowing line from Rf through g (n. 2: E and sigma of the combination, x <= 1); tangency T and Tobin's separation; complete agreement on the true distribution and a common risk-free rate make T the value-weight market portfolio.
- pp. 28-29 and n. 3: minimum-variance condition for M; beta as covariance over market variance and as each dollar's contribution to market variance; Sharpe-Lintner equation. Black version (pp. 29-30): unrestricted short sales instead of riskless borrowing; zero-beta return below the market's; without either, the market is not typically efficient and the beta relation is lost.
- p. 31 n. 4: portfolio expected return and beta are weighted averages. p. 31: individual betas imprecise; residual correlation from industry effects.
- p. 32: Jensen's time-series regression of excess returns; alpha zero for each asset. pp. 32-33, Fig. 2: beta-sorted portfolios 1928-2003, too-flat relation (8.3 predicted against 11.1 actual for the lowest beta; 16.8 against 13.7 for the highest).
- pp. 35-36: E/P, size, leverage and B/M add to beta. pp. 37-39: ICAPM; three-factor model; premia 1927-2003 of 8.3, 3.6 and 5.0 percent with s.d. 21.0, 14.6 and 14.2. p. 40: the cost of capital is forward looking. pp. 41-42, Fig. 3: Roll's critique; B/M portfolios 1963-2003.
- pp. 43-44 and n. 7: CAPM costs of equity too high for high-beta and too low for low-beta and value stocks; fund tilts produce CAPM alphas without skill; market premium 8.3 percent with s.e. 2.4.
