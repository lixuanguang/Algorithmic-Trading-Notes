## Key results

- 15-306(a): partners jointly and severally liable for partnership obligations, except as in (b) and (c); (b): no personal liability for obligations incurred before admission.
- 15-306(c): obligations arising or incurred while the partnership is an LLP, "whether arising in contract, tort or otherwise", are solely the partnership's; a partner is not personally liable "solely by reason of being or so acting as a partner".
- 15-306(e): a partner may agree to be personally liable for LLP obligations.
- 15-1001(a)-(c): a partnership becomes an LLP by stating so in its agreement (or approving the change) and filing a statement of qualification with the stated contents.
- 17-303(a): a limited partner is not liable for LP obligations unless also a general partner or it participates in control; if it participates in control it is liable only to persons who reasonably believed it to be a general partner; 17-303(b) lists activities that are not participation in control.
- 17-403(b): a general partner of an LP has the liabilities of a partner in a general partnership to third parties.
- 18-303(a)-(b): LLC debts, obligations and liabilities are solely the company's; members and managers are not personally obligated solely by reason of that status, unless they agree otherwise.

## Limits

Statutory text only; case law on veil-piercing, on partners' own torts and on other states' LLP shields (some historically limited to tort claims) was not read. That a partner remains liable for its own wrongs is an inference from "solely by reason of", marked as Interpretation in the entry.
