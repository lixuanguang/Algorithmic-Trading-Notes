## Key results

- Fund mechanics: closed-end LPs, lives 8/10/13/15 years by strategy, investment periods 3-5 years, no redemptions, secondary discounts 15-30% (up to 50-70% in crises), IPEV valuation (pp. 61-63).
- Funds of funds, mandates from about EUR 30m, co-investment rights and their adverse-selection risk; co-investment correlation with LBO funds rises with deal size (Table 3.1) (pp. 65-66, 95-98).
- IRR drawbacks: return on invested not committed capital, timing manipulation, overcommitment (pp. 68-69). Footnote 26 gives the IRR as (inflows/outflows)^(1/duration) - 1, which is an annualised multiple and equals the IRR only for a single outflow and inflow.
- Cambridge Associates statistics (US, to 2008 vintages): VC loss ratio 35.4%, growth 13.4%, LBO 15.1% (p. 116); growth pooled 19.3%, PME 1.52, quartiles 3.5%/19.1% (pp. 116-117); LBO pooled 12.8%, MOIC 1.71, PME 1.41 (p. 123); distressed pooled 11.7%, MOIC 1.56, PME 1.36 (p. 130); mezzanine median 9.7% (p. 133); direct lending 4-6% net Europe, 7-9% gross US (p. 134).
- Cycle patterns: VC after recessions, LBO after peaks, distressed countercyclical (pp. 162-163). De-smoothing called a myth (pp. 165-166).
- Denominator effect from stale private valuations (p. 101); net cash exposure 45-65% at portfolio level (p. 175) against 'at best between 40 and 60 percent' for a fund (p. 186); overcommitting 20% at 60% called gives 72% (p. 186); credit lines create call risk and contagion (pp. 175-176).
