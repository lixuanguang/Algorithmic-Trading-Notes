## Key results

- Retail trading over 60% of U.S. option volume; nearly 90% of PFOF from three wholesalers (Citadel, Susquehanna, Wolverine) by 2021Q2 (abstract; pp. 2, 11).
- U.S. options, in the authors' description of their exchange-listed OPRA data, must execute on exchanges (pp. 2, 11); wholesalers' affiliated market makers bring paired orders to price-improvement auctions; affiliated fee $0.05/contract against $0.50 for another market maker breaking up a paired order, in which responder-execution case the wholesaler nets a $0.30 rebate (p. 11, fn. 15). Listed against OTC options: FINRA Regulatory Notice 22-14 background (a 2022 proposal, not an effective rule).
- SLIM retail proxy: trades in single-leg non-ISO price-improvement auctions (OPRA flag); non-SLIM is the whole remaining pool; multi-leg auctions excluded as a noisier retail measure, so multi-leg strategy costs are not isolated (p. 12).
- Table I: quoted spread = (ask - bid)/option midpoint across exchanges; effective = 2|price - mid|/mid. SLIM 13.7% quoted, 6.7% effective; non-SLIM 11.6%, 8.6%. Weekly options 12.6%/6.6% (SLIM). ATM about 70% of trades, quoted 8.7% (SLIM)/8.4% (non-SLIM); slightly OTM 28.7%/21.3%; slightly ITM (0.1 to 1) 8.6%/5.9%, so ATM is not the lowest bin. Moneyness for calls (MidpointPrice - Strike)/Strike, opposite sign for puts; the midpoint read as the underlying's (interpretation; caption does not say) (pp. 14-17; follow-up recheck 2026-10-15 on page images).
- Aggregate SLIM trading costs to midquote $6.4bn against about $900mn commissions; 10-day-horizon net losses $2.1bn (pp. 4-5).
