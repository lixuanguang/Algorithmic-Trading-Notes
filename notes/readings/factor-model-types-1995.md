## Key results

- pp. 42-43: macroeconomic models (observed economic series; betas by time-series regression, which needs a long and stable history), fundamental models (attributes as betas; factor returns from cross-sectional regression as mimicking-portfolio returns) and statistical models (factor analysis of returns; unlabeled and rotatable); Table 1 lists inputs, estimation and outputs; without estimation error the three are rotations of one another.
- pp. 43-45: explanatory power = 1 - average specific variance / average total variance; 779 large US stocks, January 1985 to December 1993: five statistical factors 39.0 percent (the first 29.0); five macroeconomic factors 10.9 (default and term-structure factors needed); BARRA fundamental model with 55 industries and 12 indices 42.6 (industries alone 16.3).
- pp. 45-46: the macroeconomic model adds nothing to the fundamental; the fundamental slightly beats the statistical because of much more external data; by theoretical consistency and intuitive appeal the macroeconomic model is "probably the strongest".

## Limits

One sample of large US stocks from a vendor (the author was at BARRA); explanatory power is not evidence of pricing. The volume number differs between sources; Table 5's column layout is ambiguous in the scan, so only the text's conclusions are used.
