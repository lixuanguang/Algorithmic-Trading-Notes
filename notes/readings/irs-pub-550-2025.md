## Key results

- Chap. 1, Tax-Exempt Interest: "OID on tax-exempt state or local government bonds are treated as tax-exempt interest." Market discount paragraph: "Market discount on a tax-exempt bond is not tax exempt"; bought after 30 April 1993, it may be accrued and included currently as taxable interest; otherwise "any gain from market discount is taxable when you dispose of the bond".
- Chap. 1, Market Discount Bonds: market discount is the stated redemption price over basis after acquisition, treated as zero below one-fourth of 1 percent of the redemption price times full years to maturity after acquisition; with OID, the issue price plus OID accrued before purchase (for a tax-exempt bond, the OID that accrued) less basis. A bond bought at original issue is generally not a market discount bond. Accrual is ratable (daily) or, by election, constant yield.
- Chap. 4, Discounted tax-exempt obligations: OID "is generally not taxable", but for an obligation issued after 3 September 1982 and acquired after 1 March 1984 the holder accrues OID and adds it to basis to compute gain or loss.
- Chap. 4, Tax-exempt state and local government bonds: gain from market discount is usually taxable on disposition or redemption; capital gain if bought before 1 May 1993, ordinary income if after 30 April 1993; a loss is a capital loss. Example: a 20-year 6 percent bond bought at issue for 800 and redeemed after 11 years at 1,000; the OID earned while held (73) is not taxable, the unearned part (127) is capital gain.

## Limits

U.S. federal income tax for individuals only; state and local taxes, the alternative minimum tax, premium amortisation, stripped and short-term obligations and dealers are outside the entry's example. A later edition can change the guidance; recheck before relying on it.
