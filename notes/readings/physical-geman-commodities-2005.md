## Key results

- Convenience yield: a benefit that accrues to the holder of the physical commodity but not of a forward (Kaldor, Working); defined net of storage cost, so it can be negative (pp. 24-25).
- Volatility inversely related to inventories; price and volatility positively correlated - an inverse leverage effect (p. 28).
- Normal backwardation: futures downward-biased estimates of future spot; Telser found no bias in large markets; the sign of the premium depends on the commodity, inventory and maturity, as in short-dated power (pp. 33-34).
- Stocks-to-use: 540m bushels over 2.7bn = 20%, typically 20-40% (pp. 143-144). Metal ingots are halfway through the life cycle (p. 169). Electricity mostly non-storable; spikes from $30 to $1,000/MWh (pp. 251-252).
