## Key results

- Sec. 3.4: risk-free rate as the government's short-term borrowing rate; real rate = nominal - inflation (6 - 1 = 5 percent); lenders charge higher-risk borrowers a premium. No liquidity or maturity premium.
- Sec. 7.4: Fisher effect (1 + i) = (1 + R)(1 + h); 6 percent real and 2 percent expected inflation give 8.12 percent. The additive form is not called an approximation in the text.
- Sec. 10.2: bond price = PV of coupons + PV of par (2.25 percent, 15 years, 1.24 percent yield gives about 1,137.47); semiannual coupons halve the coupon and the rate and double the periods; yield found by calculator or spreadsheet; premium, discount and par bonds.
- Sec. 11.2: zero-growth value D/r (2.00 at 6 percent is 33.33); constant growth D0(1 + g)/(r - g) with r > g (5.00, 4 and 8 percent give 130.00); implied growth solved from a price (250, 10.00 and 12 percent give 7.69 percent).
- Sec. 15.1: total dollar return = dividend income + capital gain; total percent return = dividend yield + capital gain yield (TGT example, 39.20 percent); EAR = (1 + HPR)^m - 1 (14.62 percent a quarter gives 72.60 percent); arithmetic and geometric averages (CVS 2011-2020: 11.11 and 9.29 percent); the geometric is smaller unless all returns are equal; arithmetic for a typical year, geometric for compound growth.
- Sec. 2.1 (2026-10-20): sole proprietorships, partnerships, corporations, S corporations (at most 100 shareholders), LLCs and LLPs compared on liability, taxes, transferability and capital; over 80 percent of US business by sales conducted by corporations.
- Secs. 19.1-19.5 (2026-10-20): permanent and temporary working capital; cash conversion cycle (36.5 + 54.7 - 34 = 57.2 days); cost of forgoing 2/10 net 30 is 36.73 percent on a 360-day year; credit terms follow industry practice; ageing schedules; JIT and EOQ.
- Secs. 7.1-7.3 (2026-10-23): time value from the ability to earn interest; the lump sum as the basis of every stream; FV = PV(1 + r)^n with simple interest in year 1 and interest on interest after (1,000 at 4 percent: 1,040, then 1,081.60); timelines; sign convention of financial calculators; solving for N (100 to 133.82 at 5 percent: 5.97 years) and for the rate.
- Sec. 7.4 (reread 2026-10-23): PV = FV/(1 + r)^n, 'the discount rate', described as 'the inverse of the growth rate' (imprecise: the discount factor is the reciprocal of the growth factor); compounding frequency (1,000 at 6 percent: 1,060.00 annual, 1,061.36 quarterly, 1,061.67 monthly); rule of 72; Fisher example confirmed (8.12 percent).
- Sec. 8.1: perpetuity PV = C/R_s (2.00 at 7 percent: 28.57); growing perpetuity C/(R_s - G) applied to the current dividend (1.75, 5.8 and 1.2 percent: 38.04); endowment 75,000 at 3.5 percent needs 2,142,857.
- Sec. 8.2: ordinary annuity and annuity due; PVa = PYMT[1 - (1 + r)^-n]/r (25,000 for 5 years at 8 percent: 99,817.81 by factor); due = ordinary x (1 + i), 'must always be larger' (true for positive payments only when r > 0; equal at r = 0, smaller for -1 < r < 0; qualified in the entry, correction pass 2026-10-23). Table 8.2 prints year 3 as 19,846.81 (exact 19,845.81) and the total as 99,818.76 (exact 99,817.75). No growing annuity.
- Sec. 8.3: amortisation; payment from the annuity formula; monthly rate 6/12 percent over 36 months; 32,000 gives about 973.50 (text divides by "32.781" for 32.871; exact 973.5020); Table 8.4, total interest 3,046.08 (exact with the unrounded payment 3,046.07).
- Sec. 8.4: stated against effective rates; 1.5 percent a month is 19.562 percent effective; payday advance at 7 percent a week is 364 percent stated; refund-anticipation loan 26 percent simple, just over 29.3 percent compounded.
- Sec. 9.1: mixed streams valued payment by payment; annuity shortcuts do not apply.
- Sec. 16.3: IRR definition (quoted in the entry); also described as the rate at which the NPV profile 'crosses the horizontal axis' (crossing is not necessary: a double root touches the axis; qualified in the entry, correction pass 2026-10-23); accept if above the cost of capital (valid for conventional investment streams); trial and error; disadvantages: no single solution with more than one negative cash flow (-350, +950, -620; two IRRs, values not printed), reinvestment at the IRR, scale. Embroidery machine IRR 14.09 percent.
- Sec. 16.5: mutually exclusive projects by highest NPV; Table 16.11 (NPVs 2,835.62 and 3,970.67 at 9 percent; IRRs 14.10 and 13.20 percent); replacement chain and equal annuity for unequal lives; profitability index under capital rationing.
