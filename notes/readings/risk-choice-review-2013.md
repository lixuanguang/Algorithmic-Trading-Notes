## Key results

- p. 3: under expected utility, risk aversion is equivalent to concavity; first- and second-order stochastic dominance correspond to u' > 0 and u'' < 0; expected utility is not unanimously accepted and is often violated in experiments.
- p. 4 and n. 1: Pratt (1964) introduced an index of risk aversion in the small through a second-order approximation of the risk premium; Arrow developed it independently.
- p. 5: an increase in risk can raise a risk-averse investor's demand for a risky asset (two-asset example).
- pp. 13-14: a more risk-averse decision maker pays more to eliminate a risk; the risk premium for a small lottery is proportional to r(w) = -u''(w)/u'(w).
