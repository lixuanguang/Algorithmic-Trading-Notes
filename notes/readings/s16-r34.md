## Key results

- Commodity futures are derivatives, short-maturity claims on real assets, with seasonality; they insure firms' output values rather than raise capital (p. 2).
- Oil example: spot 30, future 25, expected 27; futures investor earns the 2 premium less any surprise (p. 6).
- Rolling itself has no cash flow implications (p. 6, n. 7).
- Monthly rebalanced equal weights embed a contrarian strategy that beats buy-and-hold when spikes revert (p. 9).
- Collateralised futures earned about the equity return and Sharpe ratio, with negative correlations to stocks and bonds and positive correlation with inflation (abstract).
