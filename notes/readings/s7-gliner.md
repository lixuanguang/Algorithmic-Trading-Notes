## Data and timing

Gliner's data run to 2012 and, in places, September or October 2013 (index returns, carry tables, central-bank gold, OPEC quotas); contract specifications, membership counts and market shares are as of 2011-2014.

## Key results

- Global macro: definition and aim (p. 29); four approaches, systematic underperforming discretionary in 2008 (pp. 30-33); Barclays Global Macro Index 10% a year 2002-2012 against 2% for the S&P 500 (p. 34).
- Sizing: Turtle N as a 20-day EMA of true range (pp. 50-53); 1:3 risk/reward (pp. 54-55); gap risk (p. 57); Sharpe, Sortino, drawdowns, VaR (pp. 62-66); P&L-contingent VaR utilisation (pp. 66-68); stress tests on 500 days (p. 69).
- Queries: the BoE's QE announcements in 2012, six events (pp. 79-85); analogs (p. 86).
- FX: the dollar's 85% of turnover and the DXY weights (pp. 180-181); regimes (pp. 185-194); SDR (pp. 187-188); valuation factors and carry-to-risk (pp. 195-209).
- Equities: indices (pp. 214-215); ETFs, ADRs, VIX, variance and dividend swaps (pp. 218-227); valuation and leading indicators, including AUD volatility (pp. 228-240).
- Fixed income: money markets (pp. 246-253); TIPS and breakevens (pp. 247-250); LIBOR and Eurodollar futures (pp. 254-255); swaps, fed funds, OIS and LIBOR-OIS (pp. 256-262); FRAs (p. 263); Treasury and Bund futures (pp. 264-266); curve trades, carry and rolldown (pp. 266-268); sovereign credit, CDS and the Moody's default table (pp. 269-279); ETFs (p. 280).
- Commodities: supply, demand and ending stock (pp. 293-296); curves (pp. 297-298); CRB and returns (pp. 299-300); oil, crack spread, gas (pp. 301-318); metals (pp. 319-328); agriculture (pp. 330-348).
- Central banks: goals, tools, Taylor rule, impossible trinity, money, ZLB, QE with Bauer's Table 11.4, communication, Fed, ECB, SNB and BoE (pp. 355-393).
- Data: growth, inflation, employment, external, government, consumption (the U.S. consumer at "a little more than 15 percent of global GDP", p. 416), industry, demographics with Table 12.2 (heading and source p. 424, data p. 425) (pp. 401-428).
- Sector rotation: Figure 8.3 (p. 217, an image) gives four phases: contraction (health care, telecom, consumer staples, utilities), recovery (financials, materials, consumer discretionary, technology), expansion (industrials, energy, materials, technology), slowing growth (financials, telecom, consumer staples, utilities).

## Limits

Errors and slips found while checking, each corrected in the entry named: the Fed's target is headline PCE, not core (p. 405; `def:macroeconomic-indicators`); variance swaps are not "priced with realized volatility" (p. 225; `def:vix-variance-dividend-swaps`); Treasury futures trade below the bond because of carry, not term premium (p. 264; `conv:government-bond-futures`); TIPS do not "trade at 105" when CPI is up 5% (pp. 248-249; `def:tips-breakevens`); Turtle N is not a standard deviation (pp. 52-53; `meth:macro-position-sizing-risk`); perfect correlation is not equal moves (p. 100); the printed Taylor rule has 1/(2y) for y/2 (p. 362; `mod:taylor-rule`); rolldown is misdefined (p. 268); FRA fixing date is not the trade date (p. 263); the Balassa-Samuelson effect is misdescribed (p. 205); Tobin's q is not bounded by one (p. 228); CDS upside is not the full notional (p. 279); TARGET2 is a payment system, not a securities platform (p. 387); kerosene is not diesel and Saudi Aramco is not private (pp. 301-304); platinum is not twice as dense as gold (p. 323); Chile is not 40% of copper consumption (p. 325); V2X is on the Euro Stoxx 50 (p. 224); a typo in the fed funds futures example (p. 257).

Found in the macro markets and pricing review follow-up (2026-10-16), and recorded rather than repaired in the entries: Table 10.9 (p. 322) is internally inconsistent: its ounces sum to its stated 787.0 million total, on which Mexico and China are 35.5 percent, but its printed shares imply a denominator near 594 and sum to 132.5 percent, and the text's "47 percent" and "83 percent" follow the printed shares (USGS's 2012 estimate: 33.5 percent); the four-country gold share "more than 40 percent" (p. 320) is 37.8-38.9 percent in USGS's 2011-2012 data; commercial paper is said to avoid SEC registration if its maturity is "less than 270 days" (p. 246), whereas Securities Act section 3(a)(3) says "not exceeding nine months" and has other conditions, and "most" commercial paper of 90 to 270 days contrasts with the Richmond Fed's 5 to 45 days; the VIX is described as extrapolated from implied volatilities (p. 222), not as Cboe's price-based variance; "Short-end TIPS trade like food and energy" and "TIPS are typically issued on a quarterly basis" (pp. 247-248) are his sentences. Bauer's Table 1 (FRBSF Economic Letter 2012-16) matches Gliner's Table 11.4 exactly.

Found in the second macro markets and pricing review follow-up (2026-10-16), against current primary data rather than Gliner's own sources, and recorded in the entries named: gold jewellery was about 31 percent of 2025 demand (World Gold Council), not "more than 40 percent", with investment the largest use (`def:metals-markets`); Table 12.2's demographic peaks have moved by up to decades in the UN's 2024 projections (`def:macroeconomic-indicators`); his "fixed" TRL and several "no intervention" floats are not freely floating in the Ilzetzki-Reinhart-Rogoff classification (`def:currency-regimes-intervention`); his Table 10.1 GSCI history is back-tested before 11 April 1991 (S&P), with the variant still unknown (`def:commodity-indices-risk-return`); and Bauer's 2012 QE figures sit inside a working paper whose own net-change and term-premium numbers differ from the event-day sums (`def:central-bank-policy-framework`).
