## Method

- Cross-arbitrage proxied by the dispersion of bids and of asks across venues; Nanex quote data and Waddell & Reed's own trades (printed pp. 2 and 5-6).
- Break points separate cointegrated from non-cointegrated E-mini and SPY prices: a VECM before, a VAR after (online appendix, secs. A-B).

## Data and timing

## Key results

- E-mini-SPY arbitrage "weakened at 14:15 and broke entirely at 14:44, one minute before the halt" (printed p. 7).
- Among the 50 most crashed S&P 500 stocks, those whose arbitrage broke first crashed first, with controls; the root cause of the breakdowns is not identified (printed pp. 3 and 15).
- The seller traded most of its 75,000 contracts after the breakdown; at the widest, the E-mini ask was more than 100 bp below the SPY bid; a Grossman-Miller calibration puts its overpayment at $98.6-229.8 million, two to four times quarterly operating income (printed pp. 3-4).

## Limits

Associations and a calibration, not causal identification. Working-paper version.
