\subsubsection{Equity Market Microstructure}

\begin{principle} \hlt{Equities as Microstructure Laboratory}\\
Equities are the cleanest empirical laboratory because trades, quotes, venue identifiers, and regulatory changes are comparatively well recorded. Lessons from equities transfer to other markets only after adjusting for contract standardisation, post-trade process, leverage, and dealer intermediation.
\end{principle}

\begin{caveat} \hlt{Asset-Class Transfer}\\
An equity-style CLOB is not the default architecture for every asset.\\
Futures, options, FX, fixed income, and crypto differ in tick size, margin, clearing, settlement, inventory risk, and whether liquidity is naturally multilateral or dealer-mediated.
\end{caveat}

\begin{marketrule} \hlt{Equity Block Prints and Size Priority}\\
Equity block crosses may be negotiated away from the public book but printed through an exchange or trade-reporting facility. Size-priority exceptions let very large crosses avoid being broken by small resting orders, while price-priority rules protect displayed liquidity. The design trade-off is direct: protect the book too much and blocks leave; protect blocks too much and displayed liquidity loses precedence value.
\end{marketrule}

\begin{principle} \hlt{Index Markets Trade Common Risk}\\
Index products let traders buy or sell broad market risk without trading every constituent separately.\\
They are often cheaper because index flow is less security-specific, more active, easier to net, and easier to hedge with futures, ETFs, options, or baskets.
\end{principle}

\begin{taxonomy} \hlt{Index Product Segmentation}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*.]
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item \hlt{open-end index fund}: trades with the fund at NAV; low turnover but slower intraday access;
\item \hlt{ETF}: exchange-traded claim with creation and redemption against a basket;
\item \hlt{index future}: leveraged, cash-efficient exposure with basis and roll risk;
\item \hlt{index option}: convex exposure to index level and volatility.
\end{enumerate}
Product choice is mostly a choice among trading speed, funding, tracking error, tax, and operational constraints.
\end{taxonomy}

\begin{mechanism} \hlt{ETF Basket Arbitrage}\\
Authorised participants create ETF shares by delivering the basket, or redeem ETF shares for the basket.\\
This links ETF price to constituent value, but the link weakens when creation costs, borrow, cash substitutions, taxes, or stressed constituent liquidity are material.
\end{mechanism}
