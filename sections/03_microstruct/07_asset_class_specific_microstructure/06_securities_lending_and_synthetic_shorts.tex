\subsubsection{Securities Lending and Synthetic Shorts}

\begin{definition} \hlt{Street Name}\\
Street-name securities are registered to a broker or depository while the client remains beneficial owner. This eases transfer, custody, margin, and lending.
\end{definition}

\begin{mechanism} \hlt{Securities Lending}\\
Short selling requires borrowable shares. Brokers source inventory from street-name holdings, stock-loan desks, brokers, or institutions, earning lending fees or rebate spreads.
\end{mechanism}

\begin{taxonomy} \hlt{Borrow Availability Segmentation}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*.]
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item \hlt{general collateral}: easy-to-borrow securities with low lending fees;
\item \hlt{specials}: scarce borrow with high fees and low or negative rebates;
\item \hlt{recall risk}: lender can demand the shares back, forcing replacement borrow or buy-in;
\item \hlt{settlement risk}: a failed locate or delivery can break the short-sale trade.
\end{enumerate}
\end{taxonomy}

\begin{definition} \hlt{Short Interest Rebate}\\
The short seller posts cash collateral against borrowed shares. The lender may rebate part of the collateral interest; the harder the borrow, the lower the rebate and the higher the effective shorting cost.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle} \hlt{Synthetic Short}\\
A long put and short call with the same strike and maturity replicate a short forward. It can replace scarce borrow, subject to funding, dividends, exercise, and option-liquidity costs.
\end{principle}

\begin{caveat} \hlt{Voting and Beneficial Ownership}\\
Stock lending can separate economic exposure from voting rights. Brokers and custodians need entitlement records because more investors may believe they own voting shares than there are record shares available.
\end{caveat}
